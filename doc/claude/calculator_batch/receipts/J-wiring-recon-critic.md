## disagreements

I BELIEVE recon:contract -- re-measured myself in .../critic/p3.tcl: parked at cur=0 prev=1, after calc::wave_dest the inventory reads cur=0 prev=3 (the destination), and `xschem raw switch_back` then lands ON __calc_dest1 (cur=3). After calc::wave_dest_drop the pair reads cur=0 prev=0, so the user's prev=1 is lost there too. recon:sites' account is not wrong about what the proc does, it is incomplete about what the proc leaves behind.

- DESTINATION_CONTRACT.md section 7 hazard 1 says a bare `raw switch <name>` 'answers rc 1 while silently landing on slot 0'; recon:contract says it is round-robin (cur+1) mod n. I BELIEVE recon:contract and re-measured it independently (.../critic/p4.tcl, three registered slots): rc=1 from every slot, landing 0->1, 1->2, 2->0. It lands on slot 0 only from the last slot. This matters because a restore fence written from section 7 would pass on a two-slot fixture, which is this batch's own named vacuity trap.

- recon:sites contradicts ITSELF about the size of the dutyCycle wiring: its final 'good news' item calls it 'a handful of lines with nothing to derive and nothing to decide except the name', while its own surprises list flags calc::fn_measure as A REQUIRED CHANGE SITE whose success arm is unconditional. ## vacuous_risks

- DELETING THE GUARD IS ENOUGH, AND THIS IS THE LIKELIEST VACUOUS FIRST ROW. calc::dutyCycle {v(lp)} 0.5 0 0 start ALREADY answers ok 1 today with the per-cycle series in `value` and the X series in `sweep` (measured). The row MUST assert the registered database: a non-empty db name, `xschem raw points 0` equal to the series length after switching to it, and BOTH columns read back element-wise.

- THE FIXTURE MAKES EVERY Y SERIES CONSTANT, so a constant-fill or reversed-Y producer passes everything. Measured, with `near` being a RELATIVE comparison at WDTOL 1e-7: duty on v(sq) is {0.3000000000000002 0.29999999999999966} (rel 1.8e-15 -> wd_distinct answers `same`); riseTime per edge on v(sq) is 1.600000000000014e-4, 1.5999999999999435e-4, 1.6000000000000042e-4 (rel ~4e-15); on v(lp) elements 1 and 2 agree to 9e-15. Only v(lp)'s dutyCycle at L=0.5, {0.2999385393208259 0.2999017475874794} (rel 1.2e-4), discriminates. ⚠ WD8 -- the existing END-TO-END row the batch cites as proof of the destination hand-off -- drives v(sq), so it ALREADY cannot tell a y[0]-repeated or reversed Y column from a correct one. Drive v(lp) and carry a wd_distinct non-vacuity leg.

- TWO POINTS IS THE MOST dutyCycle CAN PRODUCE, and this batch's own lesson is that two is not enough. A 2-point destination cannot catch a stride or off-by-one inside the fill loop, cannot catch a dropped middle sample, and gives the X list only two values to be in the wrong order. The only longer series available anywhere on this fixture is 3 (cross/riseTime nth 0 on v(sq) rising), which arrives with the riseTime site, not with the cheapest one -- so the first unit's row is structurally weaker than it looks and should say so rather than claim coverage.

- THE R402 HYGIENE ROWS READ AS LEAK COVERAGE AND ARE NOT. A row comparing the read-back against the verb's own list with string equality fails on a correct producer; use `near`/`wd_listcmp` with WDTOL_TIME (1e-12) for the X column and WDTOL for Y. This is the same family as the precision trap the task statement names.
