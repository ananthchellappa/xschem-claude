## What surprised me

**1. The citation split is clean enough to be a design rule, and it cuts the other way from
what I expected.** Of my ten: **5 files carry bare `file:line` citations and all 5 rotted**
(0872, 0890, 0891, 0905, 0923 — the last by ~16,586 lines). **1 file carries `file:line`
*plus a revision* (0818) and it reproduced 4 for 4** via `git show fadb226d:src/scheduler.c`.
**3 files cite only symbols — procs, rows, suite names — and all 3 resolved perfectly**
(0896, 0945, 1344). n=10 is a signal, not a rate, and I am not quoting a percentage.

The surprise is *which* files were reliable. 0945 and 1344 are among the least ceremonious in
the sample — no revision, no status apparatus, just proc names and row names — and they are
the two that survived the tree moving under them completely intact. **Precision about
coordinates is what rotted; precision about identity is what held.** That is the same
`W12b` lesson the harness batch learned (fix by identity, not by counting) arriving in the
prose layer.

**2. The most dangerous file was the one that was right.** 0905 is correctly marked FIXED,
by a batch that did the work well, and its closing section is a model of care — it answers
its own three proposed shapes one by one. And it is exactly that section that now misdirects,
because shape (2) is recorded as *deliberately rejected* while the tree runs it. **A
thorough closure written hours before a design change is worse than a thin one**, because
its detail is what earns a reader's trust. I did not expect the failure to correlate with
effort.

**3. Two files are more broken than they say, in the direction nobody checks.** 0890 calls
its defect *"latent … very likely unreachable today"* while the tree's own comment says the
failure *"CAN ACTUALLY BE SPOKEN"* and names the two rows that drive it. 1219's `grep -rn
SABOTAGE src/` — the protocol's own closing assertion, specified as *"must be empty"* —
returns **8** on a clean tree. Neither is `STALE-OPEN` in the schema's sense (neither claims
FIXED), but both understate live severity, and **the schema has no cell for that.** It has
`STALE-FIXED` (says open, is fixed) but nothing for *says latent, is live*. B1 should note
that gap; it is the direction a reader is least likely to re-check, because a file admitting
weakness reads as honest.

**4. `UNKNOWN` came out 0 and I distrust that.** Every file in this draw named a symbol or a
suite I could read. Had the draw included a file whose claim was about timing, flake rates or
a GUI pixel, the honest answer would have been `UNKNOWN` or `NEEDS-RUN`. A1/A2/A4 may see
that; a 0 across all forty would be evidence the schema is too easy, not that the corpus is
healthy.
